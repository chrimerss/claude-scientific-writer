# Phrase Bank

Every entry below appears in the source corpus. Counts are raw occurrences across ~9,400
words of first-author prose. Use these in preference to synonyms — the substitutions are what
make a draft stop sounding like the author.

---

## Connectives, by measured frequency

| Phrase | n | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `First, / Second, / Third, / Finally,` | 18 | the enumeration habit; also `firstly` mid-sentence |
| `However,` | 16 | **the** pivot; sentence-initial, always comma |
| `Although` / `Though` | 10 | leading concession before the main claim |
| `For example,` | 6 | before a concrete case |
| `For instance,` | 5 | interchangeable with the above; alternate them |
| `Additionally,` | 4 | adds a parallel result |
| `Therefore,` / `Thus,` / `Hence,` | 4 | consequence |
| `As such,` | 4 | consequence of a stated condition |
| `Furthermore,` | 3 | escalating a limitation list |
| `Moreover,` | 3 | adds weight to a claim |
| `In addition,` | 3 | neutral addition |
| `In doing so,` | 3 | links a method to what it buys you |
| `In contrast,` | 2 | the tool/approach comparison |
| `Alternatively,` | 1 | second branch of a taxonomy |
| `Nonetheless,` | 1 | rare; prefer `However,` |

Never used, and therefore off-limits: `Notwithstanding`, `Whilst`, `Conversely` (use
`In contrast`), `Indeed`, `Crucially`, `Importantly`, `Strikingly`, `Remarkably`,
`It is worth emphasizing`.

## Framing the contribution

For the closing paragraph of an introduction, these stems belong to the goal move. They must
be followed by an objectives move and then an implication sentence. See
`references/section-moves.md` P5 for the full three-move structure.

- `Here we use outputs from …`
- `Here, we define X as …`
- `In this study, hourly data from … are used to investigate …`
- `Taking advantage of the benefits of X, we quantify …`
- `Given the dense stream gage network in the US, we propose a new method using …`
- `We propose a development—the … curve—to quantify …`
- `Considering these limitations, we propose "X" as …`
- `This study for the first time generates nationwide comprehensive comparisons at …`
- `Hence, we introduce the … for the first time.`
- `The aim of this article is threefold: (a) …; (b) …; and (c) ….`
- `As results indicate, …`

## Framing the gap

- `However, quantifying … is challenging due to the lack of …`
- `Despite these well-studied impacts, large-scale assessments … remain underexplored.`
- `… has not been thoroughly investigated under climate change scenarios, although …`
- `no systematic comparisons between X and Y have been done`
- `Its validity, however, requires particular attention.`
- `This assumption is increasingly challenged by …`
- `research is far less advanced for other flood types`
- `a key gap is the development of …`
- `We hypothesize three reasons for this research gap.`

## Defining and naming

- `denoted as GR herein`
- `heretofore 'flood models'`
- `termed as the ability to …`
- `also denoted as control run; CTL`
- `The term X was introduced to measure …`
- `A study refers to a flood type derived from a journal article, where …`
- `we define X as periods when … for at least N days`

## Hedging, in the author's proportions

| Device | n | Note |
|---|---|---|
| `may` / `might` / `could` | 15 | the default hedge; one per claim, never stacked |
| `remains` / `remain` | 10 | for open problems: `remains challenging`, `remain underexplored` |
| `suggests` / `suggesting` | 9 | for inference from a result |
| `likely` | 5 | for causal attribution: `likely driven by` |
| `presumably` / `plausible` | 4 | `presumably delivers`, `A plausible explanation is` |
| `appears` / `seems` | 2 | rare; use only for genuinely equivocal readings |
| `warrants` | 2 | `warrants further exploration`, `warrants scientific exploration` |

One hedge per sentence. `may possibly potentially` is not this voice.

## Stance and evaluation

- `is challenging to measure and quantify` (`challeng-` appears 20×)
- `one of the most devastating` / `deadliest` / `most destructive` (15× family)
- `a promising opportunity to …` / `have shown promise in …` / `offering a promising
  alternative` (8×)
- `underscores the importance of …` / `Our findings highlight the need for …`
- `stands as a seminal innovation`
- `a grand challenge that warrants scientific exploration`
- `is ripe for further investigation`
- `it is clear that the synergetic use of X and Y provides …`

## Limitation and concession

- `This work by no means intends to deliver an exhaustive depiction of …`
- `There are some uncertainties in our quantification … that warrant further exploration.`
- `To be noted, we only investigated …`
- `It is noteworthy that …`
- `which may or may not be realized in the future`
- `we cannot account for …`
- `limits its ability to fully represent …`
- `Therefore, our results could serve as a basis or benchmark if not the worst-case scenario.`

## Closing on the user

- `This study calls on implementing climate-resilient mitigation measures for …`
- `This new metric has implications for hydrology and emergency responders.`
- `The results of this study can inform stakeholders and decision-makers' plans to …`
- `It can be served as a basis for adapting nationwide … planning strategies.`
- `is thus informative for decision-makers`
- `We plan to integrate … in a future work.`
- `in the hope of stimulating new research endeavors`

## Quantitative phrasing patterns

- `X are becoming 7.9% flashier by the end of the century assuming a high-emissions scenario`
- `The Southwest (+10.5%) has the greatest increase … and the central US (+8.6%) is emerging as …`
- `yield losses can reach up to 14% (Fig. 3A)`
- `on average 2.4 dB smaller than that of the DPR`
- `reduced by an average of 70% when using flood-resilient rice`
- `a significant negative correlation (r = −0.5, P < 0.05)`
- `at 5.3 and 6.4%, respectively, corresponding to production losses of 10 million and 5 million tonnes`
- `About 1.6 billion people were affected during 2000–2019, the highest figure of all disasters`

Pattern: **magnitude + unit + place + period**, in that order, with the parenthetical
carrying the signed change or the figure reference.

## Do not use

Words and constructions absent from the corpus that commonly leak in from generic academic
LLM prose:

`delve` · `leverage` (as a verb, except the single `By leveraging remote sensing technology`) ·
`robust` (as filler praise) · `paradigm shift` · `game-changing` · `unprecedented` ·
`shed light on` (used once; do not repeat) · `it is important to note that` ·
`in today's world` · `a growing body of evidence suggests` · `holistic approach` ·
`cutting-edge` · `state-of-the-art` (the author writes `state-of-the-science`) ·
`myriad` · `plethora` · `navigate the complexities` · `at the intersection of` ·
`this begs the question` · `moving forward` · `key takeaway`

Formatting habits that are wrong for this voice:

- bullet lists inside manuscript body prose (enumerate inside sentences instead)
- bold text for emphasis inside paragraphs
- rhetorical questions beyond the one framing question
- an em-dash where a period would do
